Hate speech analysis is moving from coarse binary detection to structured parsing, where systems must recover attacked targets, supporting arguments, and target-level harmfulness. Existing work remains largely monolingual, although hate expression is culturally situated and language-specific cues often break transfer across settings. SPAR-Hate addresses bilingual hate parsing with an auditor-guided multi-agent framework. It decomposes each document into clause-level subproblems, elicits role-conditioned candidates from Victim, Moderator, and Cultural Bystander agents, and resolves their conflicts through evidence-constrained arbitration before sample-level reassembly. Experiments on Chinese and English benchmarks show that the main gains appear on stricter joint structural metrics rather than target recall alone. Analysis further indicates that part of the teacher framework's structured decision process can be distilled into a smaller student model.
